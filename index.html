ABOUT else

Plastic is where the story began. Not where it ends.

else is an ecological material lab exploring what we consume, what we value, what we throw away, and what we choose to carry forward.

We turn discarded plastic into objects, workshops, stories, and questions about the way we live.

01 — WHY else EXISTS

NEW, ON A SHELF We make things to last. IN A HAND, ONCE Then use them once. IN THE BIN Then call them waste.

That contradiction became hard to ignore. Plastic just made it visible.

Not because plastic is the whole problem, but because it shows us something about the way we live.

How quickly we consume. How quickly we discard. How rarely we ask where something goes after it leaves our hands.

else began as a way to look closer and to recover what was left behind. To give material another form. And to ask whether a different relationship with consumption is still possible.

04 — THE FOUNDER

What if waste wasn't the end of the story?

Q1 · WHERE DID IT START?

I was in Portugal when I came across Ocean Patrol and started joining beach clean-ups. Walking along the beach in Baleal, I began noticing small, colourful pieces of plastic in the sand. On a beach I considered beautiful and natural, it wasn't one or two pieces. It was everywhere.

Once I started seeing the plastic, I couldn't stop seeing it. Not only on the beach, but in my everyday life and in the way I consumed.

Q2 · WHY DOES THIS FEEL PERSONAL?

From a young age, I was always exploring and travelling, looking for adventures that brought me close to nature. Those experiences showed me how disconnected we can become from nature in everyday life.

Beach clean-ups changed that. Caring for a place instead of simply passing through it felt deeply meaningful.

One thing led to another: awareness → action → a new way of consuming → and eventually a plastic recycling lab.

Q3 · WHAT SHOULD else FEEL LIKE?

Another way of consuming, more connected to nature and more conscious of the consequences of our choices. And what it feels like when awareness becomes action: people coming together to make, clean, repair, question, or stand up for something they care about.

Plastic recycling alone won't make the planet plastic-free. For me, plastic is a lens on a bigger question: how do we consume, and what happens after something leaves our hands?

The real hope is that the experience changes the way we see and consume.

Q4 · WHAT COMMUNITY DO YOU HOPE FOR?

Curious people who question the norm and aren't afraid to leave comfort behind when something needs to change. People who don't pretend to have all the answers or expect perfection from each other, but are willing to learn, make mistakes, and act together.

Some things can be changed individually, but others need to be carried together.

Q5 · WHAT DOES “SERVICE OVER EGO” MEAN TO YOU?

Humility. Not always needing to be right, recognised, or at the centre of things. Sometimes that means practical work in the background. Sometimes it means listening, helping, or simply being there.

It's about paying attention to what others might need, rather than asking what I get out of it.

This is still an experiment. Still imperfect. But maybe that is the point. else exists because I believe awareness changes behavior. And because some things are worth carrying forward.

THE FULL CONVERSATION

What was the first moment that made you think differently about plastic or waste?

I was in Portugal when I came across Ocean Patrol and started participating in beach clean-ups. It was the first time I really noticed how much plastic was lying around us.

With every clean, I became more aware. It was like a seed had been planted. Once I started seeing the plastic, I couldn't stop seeing it. Not only on the beach, but also in my everyday life and in the way I consumed.

Slowly, that awareness changed me completely.

Was there a specific place, object, or experience that started this?

I remember walking along the beach in Baleal, Portugal, paying attention to what was lying in the sand. That's when I started noticing all these small, colourful pieces of plastic scattered everywhere.

It was shocking. I had never imagined the problem was already so visible in a place like this, in Europe, on a beach I considered beautiful and natural.

Once I started looking, I realised it wasn't just one or two pieces. It was everywhere.

Why does this work feel personal to you and why?

else didn't begin with an answer. It began with paying attention.

From a young age, I spent a lot of time exploring and travelling. I was always curious and looking for adventures that would get me as close to nature as possible.

Those experiences made me realise how disconnected we can become from nature in our everyday lives.

When I started participating in beach clean-ups, caring for a place instead of simply passing through it felt deeply meaningful. It changed my relationship with nature from something I explored and enjoyed into something I also felt responsible for.

One thing led to another: awareness, action, changing the way I consumed, and eventually starting my plastic recycling lab.

What do you want people to feel when they interact with else?

I would like people to experience another way of consuming. One that feels more connected to nature and more conscious of the consequences of our choices.

But I also want people to experience what it feels like when awareness becomes action. When people come together, make something, clean something, repair something, question something, or stand up for something they care about.

Plastic recycling alone won't make the planet plastic-free. For me, plastic is a lens. It allows us to look at a much bigger question: how do we consume, and what happens after something leaves our hands?

The real hope is that the experience changes the way we see and consume.

What kind of community do you hope else becomes?

A community of curious people who question the norm and aren't afraid to leave comfort behind when something needs to change.

A community that doesn't pretend to have all the answers and doesn't expect perfection from each other.

People who are willing to learn, make mistakes, take responsibility, and act together.

Because some things can be changed individually, but others need to be carried together.

What does “service over ego” mean to you personally?

Service over ego, to me, means humility. It means not always needing to be right, recognised, or at the centre of things.

I enjoy taking care of others and contributing where I can. Sometimes that means doing practical work in the background. Sometimes it means listening, helping, or simply being there.

For me, it's about putting some attention on what others might need, rather than always asking what I get out of it.
